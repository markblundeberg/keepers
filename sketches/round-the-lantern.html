Round the Lantern sketch: the night of 21 October 1887

21 October 1887

Every light reporting in 1887, flashing its own character in real seconds while the night runs at 7½ minutes a second. The keepers' entries arrive at their printed hours, and the birds counted dead are tallied in fives at their light, and stay (a small ring where the keeper wrote "many killed" without a number). Underneath, faintly, the wind of the 20th Century Reanalysis. No wave is drawn from light to light: on the one coast where that could be tested, the night came broadside.

The night's entries

Sources: the British Association's Reports on the Migration of Birds (1879–1887), each entry linked to its page scan; light characters from Findlay (1887) and Edwards (1884); 10 m wind from the NOAA/CIRES/DOE 20th Century Reanalysis v3; coast from Natural Earth. Times are Greenwich mean time; Irish keepers' Dublin times are moved 25 minutes. The phases of the lights against one another are not known; each starts at an arbitrary point of its cycle.
