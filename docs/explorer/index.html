The keepers' nights, 1879–1887

From 1879 to 1887, keepers at about two hundred lighthouses and lightships around Britain and Ireland filled in schedules of the birds they saw for a committee of the British Association, which printed their entries by name. Here is every night of those reports. Pick a night on the calendar to see it on the chart: every light at its own rhythm, the keepers' words at the hours they printed, the birds counted dead where they fell.

Every night

Each row is one lunation, from 1879 at the top to 1887 at the bottom; new moon at both edges, full moon in the middle. A cell's brightness is that night's moonlight at midnight. Each white stroke is one station whose keeper recorded birds striking the lantern or killed.

A night

Every light on that year's lists, flashing its printed character in real seconds while the night runs at 7½ minutes a second. Tallies in fives mark the birds counted dead at a light (a small ring where a keeper wrote "many killed" without a number). Short arrows show the wind as a keeper printed it (lamp colour) and as the 20th Century Reanalysis reconstructs it (blue), each pointing the way the wind blew. Choose a light to see its station.

The night's entries

How good were the keepers?

Treated as instruments, the keepers can be tested: against a modern reconstruction of the 1880s weather, against the moon's known positions, and against each other. Every test below had its check and its null written down before it was run; the details, including what failed, are in the notes.

63% against 25%

The keepers' printed winds fall within 45° of the 20th Century Reanalysis's wind at the same place and time 63% of the time, against 25% when each is paired with the same station's wind on another day. They measured the weather.

+17° against +20°

The light-vessel crews' winds are turned about 17° from true north, against a magnetic declination of about 20° then: they read the ship's compass. The lighthouses are a mixture, some true, some magnetic, a few far off.

3.1% against 7.9%

Counted over every night of each station-month with a strike, birds struck the lantern on 3.1% of nights under a bright moon (more than half lit and up at midnight) and 7.9% of the rest. Within station and month, strike nights are half as often moonlit (odds ratio 0.49).

+0.29

When a station's keeper changed, the amount it reported changed more than when the keeper stayed, beyond season and coast (log ratio +0.29, 95% 0.11–0.47). A station's record is partly a portrait of its keeper. Whether the birds killed follow the keeper is not settled.

507 at 16 stations

The largest night in the record, 21 October 1887: 507 birds recorded killed at 16 stations, 272 of them at the South Bishop off Pembrokeshire between 11 P.M. and 2.30 A.M.

no wave

On the one coast with enough timed records, a rush of birds does not pass from light to light at any speed a bird flies. The great nights come broadside.

The data

  • records.csv: 35,945 records, one per bird kind per entry: station, date, time, count, behaviour, wind and weather as printed, each with its quote and a link to its page scan.
  • keepers.csv: the keepers named on the lists, by station and year (1887's Scottish keepers, printed only in the committee's paragraphs, in keepers_1887_scotland.csv).
  • stations/characters.csv: each light's printed character, year by year, from Findlay's directories (1879, 1887) and Edwards (1884).
  • weather/nights.csv and weather/winds.csv: every station-night with its moon and reanalysis weather, and every printed wind beside the reanalysis.
  • How it was read and checked: notes/EXTRACTION.md; the whole repository, code included, at github.com/markblundeberg/keepers.

Sources: the British Association's Reports on the Migration of Birds (1879–1887), each entry linked to its page scan on the Internet Archive; light characters from Findlay (1879, 1887) and Edwards (1884); 10 m wind from the NOAA/CIRES/DOE 20th Century Reanalysis v3; the moon from JPL's DE440s ephemeris; coast from Natural Earth.

Recorded: the words, the times, the counts, the winds as printed, the light characters as printed. Reconstructed: durations the light lists don't state (by the rule above), the phases of the lights against one another (unknown; each starts at an arbitrary point of its cycle), sunset and sunrise (computed), the reanalysis wind. Times are Greenwich mean time; Irish keepers' Dublin times are moved 25 minutes.
